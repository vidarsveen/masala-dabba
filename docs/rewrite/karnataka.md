# Karnataka regional review

Completed 22 September 2026 as one four-reading bilingual batch. This ledger records the
research, conservation decisions, dependent-content reconciliation and verification used for
the internal regional gate. It does not authorize recording or publication.

## Sources and factual decisions

- The Indian GI Registry entries for [Byadagi Chilli](https://search.ipindia.gov.in/GIRPublicSearch/Application/Details/129)
  and [Dharwad Pedha](https://search.ipindia.gov.in/GIRPublicSearch/Application/Details/80)
  establish their registered names, areas and dates. Byadagi's certificate is from 2011;
  Dharwad Pedha was filed in 2007 and registered in 2008, correcting the course's old date.
- [ICAR's 2025 finger-millet review](https://icar.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-12/October_2025%20Indian%20Farming.pdf)
  supports Karnataka's leading role and about 344 mg calcium per 100 g. The course retains the
  nutritional distinction but removes immunity to drought, insects and storage loss.
- [Karnataka Tourism's statewide cuisine survey](https://karnatakatourism.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/08/Food_karnataka.pdf)
  documents strong regional variation, including northern jowar, southern ragi, coastal seafood
  and coconut, Malenadu produce and Kodagu dishes. The readings no longer divide the state into
  one rice coast and one millet interior or make a single fat or tempering universal.
- [Karnataka Tourism's Udupi account](https://karnatakatourism.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Karnataka-Cuisine.pdf)
  supports the thirteenth-century temple tradition while distinguishing it from the later,
  widespread restaurant category. The course now treats temple cuisine as an influence rather
  than the sole origin of sambar, dosa or every Udupi restaurant.
- [UNESCO's Hampi record](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/241) supports the fourteenth- to
  sixteenth-century capital, its apogee under Krishnadevaraya, destruction after Talikota in 1565,
  extensive waterworks and continuing living temples and practices. Claims that the site was
  abandoned in one season and never inhabited again were removed.
- [Karnataka Tourism on Mysuru Palace](https://old.karnatakatourism.org/tour-item/mysuru-palace/)
  supports the 1897 fire, Henry Irwin's design and 1912 completion. Illumination is described
  without making a changing weekly schedule part of the permanent course.
- [Coffee Board statistics](https://coffeeboard.gov.in/coffee-statistics.html) establish Karnataka
  as India's leading producer, especially through Kodagu, Chikkamagaluru and Hassan. Its
  [regional guide](https://coffeeboard.gov.in/coffee-regions-india.html) supports shade cultivation
  and intercrops while presenting Baba Budan's seven beans as legend; the course no longer freezes
  a changing national percentage or treats the story as a shipment record.
- Karnataka Tourism's [Kodagu food account](https://karnatakatourism.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Karnataka-Cuisine.pdf)
  and [Pandi Curry guide](https://karnatakatourism.org/en/experiences/pandi-curry-kodava-pork-curry)
  support kachampuli and pork as Kodava associations. Sources differ on resting and fermentation
  in kachampuli production, so the course records that variation instead of declaring that no
  fermentation occurs.

Quizzes now test qualified history, safe mudde handling and Udupi migration. Glossary entries and
spice cards remove universal formulas, unsafe preservation advice and time-sensitive shop claims.
Recipes refrigerate overnight rice soaking, give controlled fermentation and discard guidance,
limit fresh coconut chutney storage, and require prompt cooling of cooked pork and leftovers.
