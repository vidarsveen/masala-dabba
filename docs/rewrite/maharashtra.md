# Maharashtra regional review

Completed 22 September 2026 as one four-reading bilingual batch. This ledger records the
research, conservation decisions, dependent-content reconciliation and verification used for
the internal regional gate. It does not authorize recording or publication.

## Sources and factual decisions

- [Maharashtra Tourism, Flavors of Maharashtra](https://maharashtratourism.gov.in/flavors-of-maharashtra/)
  supports geographic variation between coastal and inland food, including rice, coconut, kokum,
  jowar and bajra. The readings now describe shifts in emphasis instead of three sealed cuisines.
- [Maharashtra Tourism, Ganesh Chaturthi](https://maharashtratourism.gov.in/festivals/ganesh-chaturthi/)
  dates Tilak's expansion of public celebration to 1893 and associates modak with the festival.
  The false claim that every modak has twenty-one folds was corrected: some observances offer
  twenty-one modak.
- [Indian Railways, history and modernisation](https://indianrailways.gov.in/Indian%20Railways%20Whistling%20Ahead-%20%20Story%20of%20Growth%20and%20Modernisation-Booklet.pdf)
  records the first passenger train between Bombay and Thane on 16 April 1853 over 34 kilometres.
  The course retains that date while treating portable street food as an adaptation to several
  urban markets rather than a railway design specification.
- [Municipal Corporation of Greater Mumbai, Mumbai City Book](https://www.mcgm.gov.in/irj/go/km/docs/documents/Smart%20City/Mumbai%20City%20Book.pdf)
  supports the seven-island history, Koli presence, Mumbadevi connection and transfer through
  Charles II to the East India Company. The revised introduction restores Koli and East Indian
  Catholic foodways to the city's foundation.
- [UNESCO, Chhatrapati Shivaji Terminus nomination](https://whc.unesco.org/uploads/nominations/945rev.pdf)
  documents Victoria Terminus and its 1888 identity. The railway history remains, without using
  commuter scale as proof that a particular snack was engineered for a fixed eating time.
- [Encyclopaedia Iranica, Persian communities of Bombay](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/bombay-persian-communities-of/)
  and [Dinshah Irani](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/irani-dinshah/) distinguish later
  Iranian Zoroastrian migration from the older Parsi community.
- [Indian Express research on Irani cafés](https://indianexpress.com/article/research/a-feast-of-berry-pulao-how-the-irani-cafes-of-bombay-served-as-socio-economic-levelers-8263822/)
  and [IIT Bombay DSource](https://www.dsource.in/sites/default/files/resource/irani-cafes-bombay/downloads/file/irani_cafes_bombay.pdf)
  show that café proprietors also included Shia and Bahai Iranis and document the recognisable
  café room and menu. The course no longer calls all Irani café owners Zoroastrian or repeats an
  unsupported corner-plot superstition as fact.
- [Indian Express on Bombay duck](https://indianexpress.com/article/lifestyle/life-style/why-is-bombay-duck-called-so-9373187/)
  and [Word Histories' documentary survey](https://wordhistories.net/2017/12/19/bombay-duck-origin/)
  show competing etymologies and early usage. The mail-train explanation is now one disputed story.

## Changes and conservation record

| Reading | Main corrections | Material retained |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Recast goda and kala masala as variable blend families, removed a fixed phodni sequence and qualified the species and sensory role of dagad phool | Roasted blends, dagad phool, peanut, curry leaf and kokum as a connected pantry |
| 2 | Replaced a coast-versus-plateau binary with geographic emphasis, allowed bhakri shaping variation, made jaggery optional by recipe, disputed Bombay-duck etymology and removed fixed thali geometry | Ghats, millet and rice, pithla, thecha, puran poli, katachi amti, Malvani fish and solkadhi |
| 3 | Made misal's rassa variable, qualified vada-pav and pav-bhaji origin stories, tied poha method to flake thickness, varied the two Kolhapuri gravies and corrected the twenty-one-modak claim | Layered misal, pav snacks, kanda poha, pandhra and tambda rassa, Ganesh Chaturthi |
| 4 | Restored pre-colonial Koli and East Indian foodways, softened mill causation, corrected Irani community scope and made Parsi dining customs customary rather than universal | Seven islands, cotton mills, first passenger railway, Irani cafés, Parsi dishes and the Gateway chronology |

The quizzes now test qualified distinctions rather than absolutes. Glossary entries permit variation
in goda masala, phodni, bhakri and rassa. Spice cards no longer assign fixed recipes, sequences or
effects to blend families. Recipe notes identify their own method as one version: misal may use a
separate rassa, poha hydration follows flake thickness, the Ashok Vaidya account is a popular story,
and the paired Kolhapuri gravies need not always share one stock. The bilingual introduction now
names geography and Mumbai's foundational and migrant communities without erasing any of them.

## Review and verification

The English fact pass removed universals, invented mechanisms and deterministic origin stories.
The listener pass shortened every reading, named subjects in spoken prose and removed internal
page references. The Norwegian-first pass replaced literal or categorical constructions while
preserving uncertainty. The bilingual alignment pass confirmed dates, recipe distinctions,
community names, list order and all changed qualifications.

Prose lint leaves only inspected rhythm heuristics, with every sentence at or below the 35-word
ceiling. Structure, supporting-content, audio-freshness and transfer-test results are recorded in
the regional milestone in the transfer plan. Recording remains pending with the course-wide audio
stage, and no existing binary was overwritten.
