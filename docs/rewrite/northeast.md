# Northeast regional review

Completed 22 September 2026 as one four-reading bilingual batch. This ledger records the
research, conservation decisions, dependent-content reconciliation and verification used for
the internal regional gate. It does not authorize recording or publication.

## Sources and factual decisions

- [Assam Tourism on cuisine](https://assamtourism.gov.in/cuisine.php) describes khar made with
  filtered ash from banana material and a formal progression from khar towards tenga. The course
  presents that order as a recognised convention rather than a chemical law or a rule in every home.
- [UNESCO's living-root-bridge tentative-list entry](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/6606)
  supports bridges formed from *Ficus elastica* roots and maintained through Khasi community
  knowledge. Unsupported fixed construction times and five-hundred-year ages were removed.
- [ICAR's July 2025 agricultural review](https://www.icar.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-12/Indian%20Farming%20July%202025%20Special%20Issue.pdf)
  treats jhum as varied and links very short fallows with soil decline. The reading no longer
  presents one crop cycle or one environmental outcome for the region.
- A [peer-reviewed analysis of edible bamboo shoots](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3857424/)
  and a [2025 review of taxiphyllin reduction](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40179559/) support
  the cyanogenic risk in fresh shoots and the importance of processing. Recipes now require a
  prepared food product and following its label rather than treating fresh bamboo as ready to eat.
- A [review of fermented foods in northeast India](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34821430/)
  distinguishes soybean, bamboo and fish ferments including kinema, axone, soibum and ngari. The
  course no longer treats a strong smell as proof of safe fermentation or those products as synonyms.
- [Assam Tourism's tea history](https://assamtourism.gov.in/tea-and-golf.php) records Singpho tea
  knowledge before colonial estates, while the [Tea Board of India chronology](https://www.teaboard.gov.in/pdf/Circular_reg_FAO_BATIC_2024_pdf569.pdf)
  supports the 1820s discovery narrative and 1830s commercial development. The reading now credits
  local use before British plantation expansion.
- [Assam Tourism's monument history](https://tourism.assam.gov.in/portlet-sub-innerpage/historical-monuments)
  supports Sukaphaa's 1228 arrival and the 1671 Battle of Saraighat. The course separates those
  events from unsupported claims that Ahom migration introduced every rice or fermentation practice.
- Official Incredible India pages on food in
  [Shillong](https://www.prod.incredibleindia.gov.in/content/incredible-india-v2/en/destinations/shillong/food-and-cuisine.html),
  [Nagaland](https://www.incredibleindia.gov.in/en/nagaland/mokokchung/a-delicious-journey-of-culinary-delights-in-mokokchung)
  and [Imphal](https://www.incredibleindia.gov.in/en/manipur/imphal/a-culinary-journey-through-imphal)
  support the named dishes while showing that Khasi, Naga and Meitei foodways should not be collapsed
  into one regional formula.

## Changes and conservation record

| Reading | Main corrections | Material retained |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Replaced the claim that spice was absent with distinct uses of ferments, alkali, smoke, herbs and chilli; added safe handling for bhut jolokia, bamboo, smoked meat and purchased ferments | Khar, axone, kinema, ngari, bamboo shoot, bhut jolokia and hearth smoke |
| 2 | Removed a single regional plate, fixed komal-saul preparation, qualified flooding, jhum, dairy and formal Assamese meal order, and diversified Sikkim's foodways | Joha, bora and komal rice, pitika, leaf and bamboo cooking, bai and chamthong |
| 3 | Treated masor tenga, Naga pork, eromba, jadoh and Himalayan dishes as variable families; removed national-dish and religious-diet generalisations | Sour broths, fermented fish, smoked pork, bamboo, jadoh, momos and thukpa |
| 4 | Corrected the Ahom chronology, credited Singpho tea knowledge, removed fixed root-bridge ages and isolation claims, and described plantation labour and post-1947 transport without freezing cuisine | Sukaphaa, Saraighat, tea estates, root bridges, trade routes and language diversity |

Quizzes now test the qualified distinctions and handling guidance. Glossary entries and spice cards
separate the named ferments, make khar strength variable and require processed bamboo. Recipes label
substitutions as adaptations, require edible-labelled mustard oil, avoid home ash extraction and home
fish fermentation, and give refrigeration guidance. Eromba's vegetarian flag now matches its fish
ingredient in both language lists.

## Review and verification

The English fact pass removed unsupported origins, superlatives, binaries and universal practices.
The listener pass made the eight-state scope explicit and removed explanations that depended on
tables or headings. The Norwegian-first pass removed categorical translations and time-sensitive
shop claims. The bilingual alignment pass confirmed dates, names, safety guidance, uncertainty,
dish order and vegetarian flags.

Prose lint reports no sentence above the 35-word ceiling. Wiring, quiz, spice, recipe and credit
checks pass, as do all seven offline transfer tests. The site build completes at 19.6 MB with no
recordings in the worktree. The freshness check reports the expected 80 stale scripts: eight for
each of the ten completed regions. Recording remains pending with the course-wide audio stage, and
no existing binary was overwritten.
