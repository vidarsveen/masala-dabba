# Tamil Nadu regional review

Completed 22 September 2026 as one four-reading bilingual batch. This ledger records the
research, conservation decisions, dependent-content reconciliation and verification used for
the internal regional gate. It does not authorize recording or publication.

## Sources and factual decisions

- UNESCO's [Great Living Chola Temples record](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/250) dates the
  consecration of Brihadisvara to 1009–1010 and gives the vimana height as 59.82 metres. The
  introduction and history reading now say nearly 60 metres rather than 66, and no longer repeat
  the unsupported claim that suitable granite was unavailable within 60 kilometres.
- The peer-reviewed studies [Diversity and Succession of Microbiota during Fermentation of the
  Traditional Indian Food Idli](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6581174/) and
  [Bacterial diversity of traditional fermented food, Idli](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9525534/)
  document diverse, changing bacterial communities and contributions from both ingredients.
  The latter also reports yeasts in natural fermentation. The course therefore no longer says
  that two bacteria living only on urad perform the entire fermentation.
- India's tourism ministry's [Chettinad cuisine overview](https://www.prod.incredibleindia.gov.in/content/incredible-india-v2/en/destinations/chennai/food-and-cuisine/chettinad-cuisine.html)
  supports the association of pepper, star anise, kalpasi and maratti mokku with Chettinad. It
  does not establish that those ingredients are unique to the cuisine or that spices should be
  scorched. The revised course treats recipes as variable and roasts spices to fragrant brown.
- The USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service's
  [safe-temperature chart](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/safe-temperature-chart)
  gives 73.9 °C for all poultry. The Chettinad chicken recipe now calls for 74 °C in the thickest
  piece. Idli batter is refrigerated after its initial warm fermentation and includes visual and
  odour discard guidance.

The English fact and listener passes removed origins, superlatives, medical promises and fixed
household rules that the evidence did not support. The Norwegian-first pass replaced categorical
translations and time-sensitive Oslo shop claims. The bilingual comparison confirmed dates,
temperature guidance, recipe quantities, list order, image keys and vegetarian flags.

Quizzes now test mixed fermentation, refrigerated leftover batter, comfort-food language and the
uncertainty around altered Roman coins and spice routes. Glossary entries and spice cards use the
same qualified explanations. Recipes now refrigerate fermented batter, give spoilage signs, keep
spices below scorching and cook chicken to 74 °C. The misleading caper-bud photograph and duplicate
credit were removed after marathi mokku's uncertain plant identity was corrected.
