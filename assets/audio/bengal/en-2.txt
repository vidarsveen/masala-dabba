Rice and the order of a Bengali meal.

At a formal Bengali lunch, rice may anchor a sequence of dishes rather than arrive with everything at once. A bitter vegetable course can open the meal, followed by dal, vegetables, fish or meat, chutney and sweets. Everyday meals are usually shorter, and household orders vary.

The Ganges and Brahmaputra river system forms an immense delta around the Bay of Bengal. Flat lowlands, monsoon rain and extensive irrigation support rice cultivation. West Bengal remains one of India's leading rice producers, although annual rankings change and the four states grouped here do not all lie within the delta.

Rice calendars include aus, aman and irrigated dry-season boro, though timing and importance differ by district. Several harvests and many local varieties connect rice to seasons, festivals and particular dishes.

Gobindobhog is an aromatic rice associated with parts of West Bengal and used in dishes such as khichuri and payesh. Atap describes non-parboiled rice, while siddha rice is parboiled. Choice depends on household, dish, price and locality.

Watered cooked-rice dishes include Bengali panta bhat and Odia pakhala, with related practices elsewhere in the east. They may be fresh, soured or deliberately fermented and served with salt, onion, chilli, vegetables or fish. The names cover several methods rather than one recipe.

Traditional overnight preparation predates refrigeration, but cooked rice held warm at room temperature can support Bacillus cereus. This course does not instruct an uncontrolled overnight rest. Use a tested fermentation method, or cool the rice promptly, refrigerate it and add chilled water before serving.

A formal meal may follow a recognised progression. Bitter greens or shukto can come first, followed by dal, fried or stewed vegetables, fish and sometimes meat. Chutney, papad and sweets may close the meal. Menus and order vary with community, season, occasion and budget.

Custom and ideas about balancing tastes help explain the progression, but it should not be presented as a nutritional law. Rice can accompany several courses, allowing each gravy, vegetable and relish to be tasted separately.

Bhaja means fried and may refer to aubergine, potato, bitter gourd or other ingredients. In a multi-course meal it often accompanies rice and dal, adding a crisp or browned element, though it can appear in other settings too.

Rice does not have the east to itself. Luchi is a puffed bread of refined wheat flour, often served for breakfasts, celebrations or special meals with dishes such as alur dom or cholar dal. Similar breads and serving patterns cross regional borders.

In Bihar, wheat and sattu support litti chokha. Wheat dough encloses seasoned roasted-gram flour and is baked or roasted, then served with ghee and chokha, a mash that may combine roasted aubergine, potato or tomato with mustard oil and chilli.

Litti is portable and can be cooked over coals or in an oven, which suits travel, work and street vending. It is now widely sold beyond Bihar. Cool leftovers promptly and refrigerate them rather than treating the filling as shelf-stable.

Many diners eat rice and gravy with the fingers of the right hand, mixing a small portion and using the thumb to guide it. Others use cutlery. Fish bones demand care whichever method is chosen, especially with fine-boned species such as hilsa.

Eating by hand lets a diner adjust the rice-to-gravy ratio and feel for bones. It is a learned technique rather than a test of authenticity.
