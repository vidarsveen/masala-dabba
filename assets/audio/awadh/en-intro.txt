Awadh.

Lucknow's courtly cooking uses rose, kewra, saffron and whole spices with careful restraint. Under dum, a dough-sealed pot slows the loss of moisture and aroma while rice, meat or vegetables finish over gentle heat. Galouti and kakori kebabs pursue different textures, while nihari gains body from hours of cooking. The Nawabs' patronage supported these techniques, and their dispersal after the British annexed Awadh in 1856 carried the food into streets, homes, weddings and the exiled court at Metiabruz.
