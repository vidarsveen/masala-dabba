Rajasthan.

Across Rajasthan, water harvesting, herding and dryland farming helped shape a pantry of pearl millet, pulses, ghee, yoghurt and ingredients dried for later use. Mathania chilli colours laal maas, while ker berries and sangri pods turn desert plants into a valued dish. Rajput court traditions, Marwari trade networks and Jain food rules added different histories to the same regional table. The Indira Gandhi Canal later changed farming in parts of the north-west, but older foods retained their place in memory and hospitality.
